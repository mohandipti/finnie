# How Interest Income Is Taxed

This article describes general US federal rules for interest earned in taxable accounts; interest inside IRAs and 401(k)s follows the account's rules instead. State rules vary.

## Most interest is taxable

Interest is generally taxable income in the year it becomes available to you, whether or not you withdraw it. This includes interest on:

- bank savings and checking accounts, money market accounts and certificates of deposit (CDs);
- corporate bonds;
- some accounts that call their payments "dividends", such as at credit unions and savings institutions: for tax purposes these are interest.

Taxable interest is taxed at ordinary income tax rates, not the lower long-term capital gains rates.

## US Treasury interest

Interest on Treasury bills, notes and bonds is subject to federal income tax but is **exempt from all state and local income taxes**. This can make Treasuries relatively more attractive to people in high-tax states.

## Municipal bond interest

Interest on certain bonds issued by a state, the District of Columbia or a US territory to fund government operations is generally **not taxable at the federal level**. It must still be reported on the tax return. Whether a state taxes it depends on the state's own rules.

**Example:** at a 24% federal marginal rate, a municipal bond paying 3% tax-free gives the same after-tax income as a taxable bond paying about 3.95% (3% ÷ (1 − 0.24)), before state taxes. This is called the **tax-equivalent yield**.

## US savings bonds

For Series EE and Series I savings bonds, you can choose to report the interest each year, but generally don't have to until the bonds mature or are cashed in. Interest may be excluded entirely when the bonds are used for qualified higher education expenses, if the program's requirements are met.

## Form 1099-INT

Banks and brokers generally send Form 1099-INT when you receive $10 or more of interest. All taxable and tax-exempt interest must be reported, even without a form.

## Key takeaways

- Interest from banks, CDs and corporate bonds is taxed as ordinary income.
- Treasury interest is federally taxable but free of state and local income tax.
- Qualifying municipal bond interest is generally free of federal tax but still reported.
