# Investment Fraud Red Flags and Ponzi Schemes

## Why fraud is worth learning about

Investment fraud often targets people who are new to investing, using pitches that sound exciting and safe at the same time. Recognizing the warning signs is one of the most useful protections an investor has.

## Common red flags

The SEC's checklist highlights these warning signs:

- **Unlicensed** investment professionals, or sellers with exaggerated or false credentials.
- Offers that sound **"too good to be true."**
- **"Risk-free"** opportunities and promises of **guaranteed returns** or great wealth.
- **"Everyone is buying it"** pitches.
- **Pressure to invest right now.**
- Sensational pitches, sometimes with fake testimonials.
- Unsolicited messages asking for **personal information**.
- Requests to pay by **credit card, gift card, or wiring money** abroad or to a personal account.

## Ponzi schemes

A **Ponzi scheme** pays existing investors with money collected from **new investors**, rather than from real investment earnings. Organizers often promise high returns with little or no risk, but in many cases the money is never invested at all. Because there are few or no legitimate earnings, the scheme needs a constant flow of new money and eventually collapses.

The name comes from Charles Ponzi, who defrauded investors in the 1920s with a postage-stamp scheme.

Warning signs include **guaranteed** returns, returns that are **unusually consistent** regardless of market conditions, investments **not registered** with the SEC or state regulators, secretive or overly complex strategies, missing paperwork, and difficulty withdrawing money.

## How to protect yourself

1. **Ask questions** and get answers in writing.
2. **Research** every opportunity thoroughly before investing.
3. **Check the seller's background:** investment professionals' registration and disciplinary history can be searched at Investor.gov.

## Key takeaways

- Real investments carry risk; "guaranteed high returns" are a hallmark of fraud.
- Pressure, secrecy and unusual payment methods are major warning signs.
- Verify that both the investment and the seller are properly registered.
