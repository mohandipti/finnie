# Standard Deduction vs. Itemized Deductions

This article describes general US federal rules for tax year 2026. Amounts and limits change; check IRS.gov.

## What deductions do

Deductions **reduce your taxable income**, the income your tax brackets apply to. Each year you generally choose one of two options: take the **standard deduction** or **itemize** your deductions.

## The standard deduction

The standard deduction is a **flat amount** based on your filing status, adjusted every year for inflation. For 2026 it is:

- **$16,100** for single filers;
- **$32,200** for married couples filing jointly.

People aged 65 or older, and people who are blind, get an additional amount. Some taxpayers can't use it, for example a married person filing separately whose spouse itemizes.

## Itemized deductions

Itemizing means listing specific deductible expenses on **Schedule A**. Common ones include:

- mortgage interest on a home;
- state and local taxes, up to a limit;
- charitable donations;
- medical and dental expenses above a percentage of income.

Each comes with its own rules and limits, explained in the Schedule A instructions.

## Which to choose

Itemize if your allowable itemized deductions add up to **more than your standard deduction**; otherwise, the standard deduction gives the bigger tax reduction. Most taxpayers take the standard deduction.

**Example (single filer, 2026):** mortgage interest of $9,000, state and local taxes of $6,000 and charitable gifts of $2,000 total $17,000. That's more than the $16,100 standard deduction, so itemizing reduces taxable income by an extra $900. In the 22% bracket, that saves about $198 of tax. Without the mortgage, the total would be $8,000, and the standard deduction would be better.

## Bunching

Some people "bunch" deductible expenses such as charitable gifts into alternate years, itemizing in one year and taking the standard deduction in the next, to get more total benefit.

## Key takeaways

- Deductions reduce taxable income; you choose either the standard deduction or itemizing.
- The 2026 standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers and $32,200 for married couples filing jointly.
- Itemize only if your deductible expenses exceed the standard deduction.
