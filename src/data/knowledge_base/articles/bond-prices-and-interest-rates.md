# Bond Prices, Yields and Interest Rate Risk

## The key rule: prices and rates move in opposite directions

When market interest rates go **up**, the prices of existing fixed-rate bonds go **down**, and when rates fall, their prices rise. This is **interest rate risk**, and it affects all fixed-rate bonds, even US Treasury bonds.

The reason: a bond's **coupon** (its fixed interest payment) doesn't change. If new bonds pay more, an older bond paying less is worth less to a buyer, so its price falls until its return matches the new bonds.

## Yield to maturity

A bond's **yield to maturity** is what an investor would earn by buying at today's price and holding until the bond matures. Price and yield move in opposite directions: as the price falls, the yield for a new buyer rises.

**Examples (from the SEC), a 10-year Treasury bond with a 3% coupon and $1,000 face value:**

| A year later, market rates... | Bond price | Yield to maturity |
|---|---|---|
| fall from 3% to 2% | about $1,082 | 2% |
| rise from 3% to 4% | about $925 | 4% |

If you hold the bond to maturity, you still receive the full $1,000 face value (assuming the issuer doesn't default); the price changes matter if you sell early, and they show up in the value of bond funds.

## What makes a bond more sensitive

- **Longer maturity:** bonds that mature further in the future have more interest rate risk than similar shorter-term bonds.
- **Lower coupon:** with everything else equal, a bond with a 2% coupon falls more when rates rise than one with a 4% coupon.

This is why interest rate risk matters most when buying long-term bonds at low interest rates.

## Duration

**Duration**, measured in years, combines maturity, coupon and the timing of payments into one measure of rate sensitivity. As a rule of thumb, a bond or bond fund's price changes by about its duration in percent for each 1-percentage-point change in rates.

**Example:** a bond fund with a duration of 6 years would be expected to lose about 6% if rates rose by 1 percentage point, and gain about 6% if they fell by 1 point. Bond funds report their duration in their fact sheets.

## Key takeaways

- When interest rates rise, existing bond prices fall, and vice versa.
- Longer maturities and lower coupons mean more interest rate risk.
- Duration estimates how much a bond's price changes for a 1-point move in rates.
