# Index Funds and Passive Investing

## What an index fund is

An **index fund** is a mutual fund or ETF that follows a **passive** strategy: it aims to match the return of a particular market index, before fees, by investing in the securities in that index. An S&P 500 index fund, for example, holds the stocks in the S&P 500 in roughly the same proportions.

## Passive vs. active management

- **Index (passive) funds** don't try to beat the market; they try to be the market.
- **Actively managed funds** aren't tied to an index. Their managers buy and sell investments, sometimes daily, aiming to beat a benchmark or meet another objective.

## Why investors use index funds

Investor.gov notes that passive management usually means:

- **lower fees** than actively managed funds;
- **less trading**, which means lower transaction costs;
- **better tax efficiency**, because fewer realized capital gains are passed on to shareholders;
- **broad diversification**, since one fund can hold hundreds or thousands of companies.

Costs matter because they come straight out of returns, every year.

**Example:** on a $10,000 investment growing 7% a year before fees, a fund charging 0.05% a year would be worth about $75,000 after 30 years, while one charging 1.00% would be worth about $57,000. The difference is roughly $18,000, purely from fees.

## What to keep in mind

- An index fund follows its index **down** as well as up; it won't sidestep a falling market.
- Indexes differ: a total-market fund, an S&P 500 fund and a technology-sector fund are all "index funds" with very different risks.
- Funds tracking the same index can still differ in fees and in how closely they track it (**tracking error**).
- Index ETFs trade throughout the day, with bid-ask spreads; index mutual funds trade once a day at their net asset value.

## Key takeaways

- Index funds aim to match an index's return, not beat it.
- They usually have lower fees, lower trading costs and better tax efficiency than active funds.
- Check which index a fund tracks and its expense ratio before investing.
