# Mutual Fund Share Classes and Sales Loads

## Same fund, different costs

Many mutual funds offer several **share classes**. Each class invests in the same portfolio but has a different fee structure. Picking the right one can depend on how much you invest, how long you'll hold it and how you buy it.

Some classes charge **sales loads** (sales charges) when you buy, when you sell, or both. These are on top of the fund's ongoing operating expenses, which include **12b-1 fees** (for marketing, distribution and sometimes paying financial professionals).

## Common classes

- **Class A:** usually a **front-end sales load**, a percentage of your purchase taken when you buy, which may be reduced for larger investments. They also have a 12b-1 fee, typically lower than other load classes. Some investors, such as those in advisory accounts, may get the load waived.
- **Class C:** usually no front-end load, but a higher 12b-1 fee and a **contingent deferred sales load (CDSL)**, charged if you sell within a set period, often a year. Because the higher yearly fee continues, Class C can cost more if held for a long time.
- **Class B:** once common, with a CDSL and higher 12b-1 fee but no front-end load; they're no longer widely available.
- **No-load classes:** no sales charge, typically available directly through a brokerage account, the fund company or an advisory account. They still have operating expenses.
- **Retirement classes:** available only through employer plans such as 401(k)s.

**Example:** a $10,000 purchase of Class A shares with a 5% front-end load means $500 goes to sales charges and $9,500 is invested. Class C shares might invest the full $10,000 but charge, say, an extra 0.75% a year, which over a long holding period can add up to more than the Class A load.

## Breakpoint discounts

Funds with front-end loads often charge less for bigger investments. These **breakpoints** may apply when:

- you invest a large amount at once;
- **rights of accumulation**: what you buy plus what you (and eligible family members) already hold in the fund family reaches a threshold;
- a **letter of intent**: you commit to investing a set amount over a period.

Ask your broker which breakpoints you qualify for; missing them means paying more than necessary.

## Watch for conflicts

Sales loads and 12b-1 fees usually compensate the financial professional who sells you the fund, and they may earn more from some classes than others. Compare total costs in each class's prospectus fee table.

## Key takeaways

- Share classes hold the same investments but charge different fees.
- Class A has an up-front load; Class C has higher yearly fees and an early-sale charge.
- Ask about breakpoint discounts and compare total costs over your expected holding period.
