# Bull Markets, Bear Markets and Corrections

## Bull and bear markets

- A **bull market** is a period of rising prices and optimism. Generally, it describes a rise of 20% or more in a broad market index.
- A **bear market** is a period of falling prices and pessimism. Generally, a decline of 20% or more in a broad market index meets the threshold.

These are rules of thumb used by investors and the media, not official definitions.

## Corrections

A **correction** is a reversal of at least 10% before the previous trend resumes. It can technically be a rise or a fall, but it usually refers to a drop.

**Example:** the S&P 500 rises from 4,000 to 5,000, then falls to 4,400. That's a 12% decline from the peak, so it's a correction. If it fell below 4,000, a 20% drop from 5,000, it would be a bear market.

## Volatility

**Volatility** is how widely prices swing over a short time. The Cboe Volatility Index (VIX) measures investors' expectations of major moves in US stocks. Volatile markets can inspire fear, and FINRA cautions against impulsive decisions when markets turn.

## Market-wide circuit breakers

To slow panic selling, US markets have **circuit breakers** based on the S&P 500's fall from the previous day's close:

- a **7% drop** (Level 1) or **13% drop** (Level 2) before 3:25 p.m. halts trading on all stock and futures exchanges for 15 minutes;
- a **20% drop** (Level 3) at any time stops trading for the rest of the day.

Individual stocks also have **limit up/limit down** bands that pause trading when a price moves too far, too fast.

## Living through a bear market

Bear markets are a normal, if painful, part of investing. Selling in a panic can lock in losses and mean missing the recovery. Having an emergency fund, a diversified portfolio and a time horizon matched to your goals makes downturns easier to sit through.

## Key takeaways

- Bull and bear markets are generally defined by 20% moves in a broad index; corrections by 10%.
- Circuit breakers pause trading when the S&P 500 falls 7%, 13% or 20% in a day.
- Downturns are normal; decisions made in a panic are often costly.
