# Rolling Over a 401(k) or IRA

## What a rollover is

A **rollover** moves money from one retirement plan or IRA into another without paying tax on it. People often roll over a 401(k) when they leave a job, either into the new employer's plan or into an IRA. If you take the money as cash instead, it is taxable (except qualified Roth money and amounts already taxed), and you may owe an additional **10% tax** on early distributions unless an exception applies.

## Three ways to move the money

- **Direct rollover:** your plan pays the money straight to another plan or IRA (sometimes as a check payable to the new account). Nothing is withheld for tax.
- **Trustee-to-trustee transfer:** for IRAs, the institution sends the money directly to another IRA or plan. No tax is withheld.
- **60-day (indirect) rollover:** the money is paid to you, and you have **60 days** from receiving it to deposit it in another plan or IRA.

## The 20% withholding trap

A distribution from an employer plan **paid to you** has **20% withheld** for federal tax, even if you plan to roll it over. To roll over the full amount, you must make up the withheld 20% from other money within the 60 days; otherwise the missing part is treated as a taxable distribution.

**Example:** you leave a job with $50,000 in a 401(k) and take a check. You receive $40,000 ($10,000 is withheld). To avoid tax on the whole amount, you must deposit $50,000 into an IRA within 60 days, using $10,000 of your own savings, and you get the $10,000 back as a tax credit when you file. A direct rollover avoids all this.

## Other rules

- **One per year for IRAs:** you can make only one IRA-to-IRA 60-day rollover in any 12-month period, counting all your IRAs together. Direct trustee-to-trustee transfers don't count toward this limit.
- **Missed the 60 days?** The IRS may waive the deadline in certain situations beyond your control.

## Before rolling over

Compare fees and investment choices in the old plan, the new plan and an IRA. Some employer plans offer low-cost funds or other protections, so leaving the money where it is can also be a reasonable option.

## Key takeaways

- A direct rollover or trustee-to-trustee transfer moves retirement money without withholding.
- If a plan pays you directly, 20% is withheld, and you have 60 days to roll over the full amount.
- Cashing out is usually taxable and may add a 10% early-distribution tax.
