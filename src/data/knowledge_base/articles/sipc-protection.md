# SIPC Protection for Brokerage Accounts

## What SIPC does

The Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC) protects customers if a SIPC-member brokerage firm fails financially and customers' cash or securities are missing. In a liquidation, SIPC works to return the securities and cash that were in customers' accounts, replacing missing securities when possible.

## How much is protected

The limit is **$500,000 per customer, including up to $250,000 for cash**. The cash must be held in the account in connection with buying or selling securities. Customers don't need to be US citizens or residents.

## What counts as a security

SIPC protects stocks, bonds, Treasury securities, certificates of deposit, mutual funds, money market mutual funds and certain other investments. Money market mutual funds, often thought of as cash, are protected as securities.

It does **not** protect commodity futures contracts (outside special portfolio margining accounts), foreign exchange trades, investment contracts and fixed annuities that aren't registered with the SEC, or digital and crypto assets that don't qualify as registered securities, even when held at a SIPC-member firm.

## What SIPC does not protect against

SIPC protects the **custody** of your assets, not their value. It does not cover:

- a drop in the market value of your investments;
- being sold worthless stocks or other securities;
- losses from a broker's bad advice or unsuitable recommendations.

That's the key difference from FDIC insurance on bank deposits: FDIC protects the dollar amount of a deposit, while SIPC only restores missing securities and cash.

**Example:** your brokerage firm fails while your account holds 100 shares of a fund and $10,000 in cash. SIPC works to return the 100 shares and the $10,000. If the fund's price had fallen 20% before the failure, SIPC doesn't make up that market loss.

## Key takeaways

- SIPC covers up to $500,000 per customer, including $250,000 of cash, when a member brokerage firm fails.
- It replaces missing securities and cash; it doesn't protect against investment losses.
- Losses from bad advice or fraud in what you were sold are a separate matter, handled through regulators or arbitration.
