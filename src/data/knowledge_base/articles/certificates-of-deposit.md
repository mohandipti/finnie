# Certificates of Deposit (CDs)

## What a CD is

A certificate of deposit (CD) is a savings account that holds a fixed amount of money for a fixed period, such as six months, one year or five years. In exchange, the bank pays interest. When the CD **matures**, you get back your original deposit plus interest. CDs from federally insured banks are covered by FDIC deposit insurance, making them one of the safest savings options.

## What to check before buying

The CD's disclosure should state:

- the interest rate, and whether it is fixed or variable;
- when interest is paid (for example monthly or semi-annually) and how;
- the maturity date;
- the penalty for withdrawing early, which can wipe out some of the interest earned.

**Example:** a 1-year CD might charge a penalty of several months' interest if you cash it in early. If you might need the money sooner, a shorter CD or a savings account may fit better.

## Brokered CDs

Brokerage firms and other "deposit brokers" also sell CDs, called **brokered CDs**. They can be more complex than CDs bought directly from a bank:

- **Insurance limits:** FDIC coverage is $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category. A brokered CD at a bank where you already have deposits could push you over the limit.
- **Call features:** some can be "called" (redeemed early) by the bank, but you don't have the same right. Calls tend to happen when rates fall, leaving you to reinvest at lower rates.
- **Simple interest:** brokered CDs generally pay interest out in cash instead of compounding it.
- **Selling early:** instead of an early withdrawal penalty, you usually sell on a secondary market, where the price may be less than you paid, especially if rates have risen, plus a sales fee. Some have no secondary market at all.
- **Long maturities:** terms can run from six months to thirty years.

Deposit brokers aren't licensed or approved by any agency, so check their background and the issuing bank.

## Key takeaways

- CDs pay interest for locking money away for a fixed term, and bank CDs are FDIC-insured.
- Early withdrawal penalties apply, so match the term to when you'll need the money.
- Brokered CDs may be callable, pay simple interest and lose value if sold before maturity.
