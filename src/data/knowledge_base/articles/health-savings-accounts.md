# Health Savings Accounts (HSAs)

This article describes general US federal rules; limits are adjusted every year and some states tax HSAs differently. Check IRS.gov for current figures.

## What an HSA is

A health savings account (HSA) is a tax-advantaged account for paying medical expenses. It belongs to you, not your employer: it is **portable**, so it stays with you if you change jobs or leave the workforce, and unused money carries over from year to year.

## Who can open one

To contribute to an HSA, you must:

- be covered by a **high deductible health plan (HDHP)**;
- have no other disqualifying health coverage;
- not be enrolled in Medicare; and
- not be claimed as a dependent on someone else's tax return.

For 2025, an HDHP must have a deductible of at least $1,650 for self-only coverage or $3,300 for family coverage.

## Three tax advantages

HSAs are often described as having a "triple" tax advantage:

1. **Contributions** are deductible, even if you don't itemize deductions (contributions through an employer's payroll are made before tax).
2. **Earnings** aren't taxed while they stay in the account; many HSAs let you invest the balance in funds.
3. **Withdrawals** used for qualified medical expenses aren't taxed.

## Contribution limits

| Year | Self-only coverage | Family coverage |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | $4,300 | $8,550 |
| 2026 | $4,400 | $8,750 |

People aged 55 or older can contribute an extra $1,000 a year.

## Non-medical withdrawals

Money withdrawn for anything other than qualified medical expenses is taxed as income and may also face an **additional 20% tax**. The additional tax doesn't apply after you become disabled, reach age 65 or die; after 65, non-medical withdrawals are taxed as income, much like a traditional IRA.

**Example:** someone under 65 withdraws $1,000 for a non-medical purpose. They owe income tax on the $1,000 plus a $200 additional tax.

## Key takeaways

- An HSA needs a high deductible health plan and no other disqualifying coverage.
- Contributions, growth and qualified medical withdrawals can all be tax-free.
- The account is yours to keep, and unused money carries over.
