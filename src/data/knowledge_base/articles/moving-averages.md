# Moving Averages Explained

## What a moving average is

A moving average smooths out daily price swings by averaging a stock's closing price over a set number of days. A 50-day moving average is the average closing price of the last 50 trading days; each day, the newest price is added and the oldest dropped, so the average "moves".

## Why people look at them

Daily prices are noisy. A moving average makes the underlying direction, or trend, easier to see:

- Price **above** a rising moving average is often described as an uptrend.
- Price **below** a falling moving average is often described as a downtrend.

## Common periods

- **50-day:** a medium-term trend.
- **200-day:** a long-term trend, widely followed for broad indexes like the S&P 500.

Shorter averages react quickly to price changes; longer averages react slowly and filter out more noise.

## Crossovers

- A **golden cross** happens when the 50-day average rises above the 200-day average.
- A **death cross** happens when the 50-day average falls below the 200-day average.

These are popular descriptions of trend changes, but they look backward. Moving averages describe what prices have done, not what they will do, and crossovers frequently give false signals.

## Simple vs. exponential

A simple moving average (SMA) weights every day equally. An exponential moving average (EMA) gives more weight to recent days, so it responds faster to new price moves.

## Key takeaways

- A moving average is the average price over a recent window of days.
- It helps describe a trend by smoothing out daily noise.
- It is a backward-looking description, not a prediction.
