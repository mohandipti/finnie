# The Net Investment Income Tax (NIIT)

This article describes the general US federal rule for individuals; estates and trusts have their own thresholds.

## What it is

The **net investment income tax (NIIT)** is an extra **3.8%** federal tax on investment income for people with higher incomes. It is in addition to regular income tax and capital gains tax.

## Who pays it

It applies when **modified adjusted gross income (MAGI)** is above these thresholds:

| Filing status | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Married filing jointly, or qualifying surviving spouse | $250,000 |
| Single or head of household | $200,000 |
| Married filing separately | $125,000 |

These thresholds are set in law and are **not adjusted for inflation**, so more people become subject to the tax over time.

## How much

The tax is 3.8% of the **lesser** of:

- your net investment income, or
- the amount by which your MAGI exceeds the threshold.

**Example:** a single filer has MAGI of $230,000, including $40,000 of investment income. MAGI exceeds the $200,000 threshold by $30,000, which is less than the $40,000 of investment income. The NIIT is 3.8% × $30,000 = **$1,140**.

## What counts as investment income

Net investment income generally includes:

- interest, dividends, certain annuities, royalties and rents;
- capital gains from selling stocks, bonds, mutual funds and real estate;
- income from passive business activities and from trading financial instruments.

It does **not** include:

- wages, unemployment compensation or income from an active business;
- tax-exempt municipal bond interest;
- the excluded part of a gain from selling your main home;
- distributions from IRAs and most retirement plans (although they can raise MAGI, which affects whether investment income is taxed).

## Key takeaways

- The NIIT is an extra 3.8% tax on investment income for higher earners.
- It applies above $200,000 of MAGI for single filers and $250,000 for married couples filing jointly.
- Wages, municipal bond interest and retirement account distributions aren't investment income for the NIIT.
