# Dividends, Dividend Yield and Ex-Dividend Dates

## What dividends are

When companies are profitable, they can share some earnings with shareholders as **dividends**, usually in cash, often quarterly. You can take the cash or reinvest it to buy more shares, for example through a **dividend reinvestment plan (DRIP)**.

Dividends on common stock are never guaranteed: a company can cut or stop them at any time. Companies known for steady dividends are sometimes called income stocks; fast-growing companies often pay none and reinvest their profits instead.

## Dividend yield

**Dividend yield** is the yearly dividend per share divided by the share price.

**Example:** a stock priced at $50 that pays $0.50 a quarter ($2 a year) has a dividend yield of 4%.

Because the price is in the denominator, the yield rises when the price falls. An unusually high yield can be a warning sign that investors expect the dividend to be cut.

## Key dates

1. **Declaration date:** the company announces the dividend, its amount and the dates below.
2. **Record date:** you must be on the company's books as a shareholder on this date to receive the dividend.
3. **Ex-dividend date:** set by stock exchange rules, usually the **same day as the record date** (or the business day before, if the record date isn't a business day). If you buy **on or after** the ex-dividend date, the seller gets the dividend; buy **before** it, and you get it.
4. **Payable date:** the dividend is paid.

**Example (Investor.gov):** a company declares a dividend on Monday, March 2, with a record date of Monday, March 16 and payment on March 17. The ex-dividend date is March 16, so anyone who buys on March 16 or later doesn't receive this dividend.

## The price adjusts

On the ex-dividend date, the share price usually drops by about the dividend amount, since new buyers no longer get it. Buying just before the ex-date to "capture" a dividend doesn't create free money, and the dividend is taxable.

## Key takeaways

- Dividends share profits with shareholders but can be cut at any time.
- Dividend yield = yearly dividend ÷ share price.
- To receive a dividend, you must buy before the ex-dividend date.
