# US Treasury Bills, Notes and Bonds

## What Treasury securities are

Treasury securities are loans to the US government. They are backed by the full faith and credit of the United States, so they are considered to have very low credit risk. Interest is subject to federal income tax but **exempt from state and local income taxes**.

The Treasury offers five types of **marketable** securities, which can be bought and sold before maturity: bills, notes, bonds, Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) and Floating Rate Notes (FRNs). It also sells **non-marketable** savings bonds, which are registered to one person and can't be sold to anyone else.

## Bills, notes and bonds

| Type | Term | How you earn |
|---|---|---|
| **Treasury bills** | 4 to 52 weeks | Bought at a discount (or at face value); you receive face value at maturity |
| **Treasury notes** | 2, 3, 5, 7 or 10 years | Interest every six months |
| **Treasury bonds** | 20 or 30 years | Interest every six months |

**Example:** you buy a 26-week bill with a $1,000 face value for $980. At maturity you receive $1,000; the $20 difference is your interest.

**Floating Rate Notes** have 2-year terms and pay interest quarterly at a rate that resets with 13-week bill rates. **TIPS** adjust their principal for inflation.

## How to buy

Treasuries are sold at auction. You can buy them directly from the government through **TreasuryDirect.gov**, with a minimum purchase of $100, or through a bank or broker, which also lets you buy and sell existing Treasuries on the secondary market. Many investors own Treasuries indirectly through bond funds.

## Risks

Treasuries have very little credit risk, but they aren't risk-free:

- **Interest rate risk:** if you sell a note or bond before maturity after rates have risen, you may get less than you paid. Longer terms are more sensitive.
- **Inflation risk:** a fixed payment buys less if inflation runs higher than expected (TIPS address this).
- **Reinvestment risk:** when a short-term bill matures, rates may be lower.

## Key takeaways

- Treasury bills, notes and bonds are US government debt with very low credit risk.
- Bills are short-term and sold at a discount; notes and bonds pay interest every six months.
- Interest is federally taxable but free of state and local income tax.
