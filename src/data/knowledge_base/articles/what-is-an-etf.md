# What Is an ETF?

## A basket of investments that trades like a stock

An exchange-traded fund (ETF) is a fund that holds a collection of investments, such as stocks or bonds, and trades on a stock exchange like a single stock. Buying one share of an ETF gives you a small slice of everything the fund holds.

**Example:** An S&P 500 ETF holds shares in roughly 500 large US companies. One purchase gives you exposure to all of them.

## How ETFs differ from mutual funds

- **Trading:** ETFs trade throughout the day at market prices; mutual funds are bought and sold once a day at the end-of-day price.
- **Minimums:** ETFs can be bought one share at a time (or in fractions at some brokers); some mutual funds require a minimum investment.
- **Costs and taxes:** many ETFs have low expense ratios, and their structure often makes them more tax-efficient in taxable accounts.

## Types of ETFs

- **Broad market index ETFs:** track a whole market, such as the S&P 500 or the total US stock market.
- **Bond ETFs:** hold many bonds.
- **Sector ETFs:** focus on one industry, such as technology or healthcare. These are less diversified.
- **International ETFs:** hold companies outside the US.

## Costs to understand

- **Expense ratio:** the annual fee, as a percentage of your investment. A 0.05% expense ratio costs $5 a year per $10,000 invested.
- **Bid-ask spread:** the small difference between buying and selling prices, usually tiny for popular ETFs.
- **Brokerage commissions:** some brokers charge a fee to trade, although many now offer commission-free ETF trading.
- **Premiums and discounts:** an ETF's market price can be slightly above (a premium) or below (a discount) the value of its holdings, its net asset value (NAV).

## Risks

An ETF is only as diversified as what it holds. A broad index ETF spreads risk across hundreds of companies; a narrow sector ETF concentrates it. ETF prices still rise and fall with the markets they track.

## Key takeaways

- ETFs bundle many investments into one tradable share.
- Broad index ETFs offer instant diversification at low cost.
- Check what an ETF holds and its expense ratio before comparing funds.
