# Budgeting Basics: Making a Spending Plan

## Why budget

A budget shows how much money comes in, where it goes, and what's left over. Without that picture, it's hard to know whether you can cover your expenses and still save for goals such as an emergency fund, a car, a home down payment or retirement. Budgeting is also a key step in getting debt under control.

## Four steps to a budget

The CFPB suggests four steps:

1. **Where does my money come from?** Record all income: wages, self-employment, child support, government benefits and any other sources.
2. **Where does my money go?** Log your spending by category, such as housing, utilities, groceries, eating out and entertainment. If that feels overwhelming, start with one week of receipts or statements.
3. **What are my bills and when are they due?** If you run short at the end of the month, the timing of bills and paychecks may not match. Missing payments can also hurt your credit scores.
4. **Create a working budget.** Combine income, spending and due dates into one plan, and update it when your job or spending habits change.

## The 50/30/20 rule

A common rule of thumb, used in the CFPB's teaching materials, splits take-home (after-tax) pay three ways:

- **50% for needs:** housing, utilities, groceries, insurance, minimum debt payments;
- **30% for wants:** dining out, entertainment, travel;
- **20% for savings and goals:** emergency fund, retirement, extra debt payments.

**Example:** with $4,000 a month of take-home pay, that's about $2,000 for needs, $1,200 for wants and $800 for savings. It's a starting point, not a rule everyone must follow; in high-cost areas, needs may take a larger share.

## Sticking to it

- Use a tracking method that suits you: an app, a spreadsheet or a weekly receipt folder.
- Look for areas to cut back, and plan for impulse spending if you know you're prone to it.
- Set a goal, and reward yourself for progress.
- Find support from family or friends.

## Key takeaways

- A budget starts with knowing your income, your spending and when bills are due.
- The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to balance needs, wants and savings.
- Review the budget regularly and adjust it when your situation changes.
