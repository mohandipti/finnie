# The Rule of 72

## A quick way to estimate doubling time

The **Rule of 72** estimates how long it takes money to **double** with compound growth: divide 72 by the yearly rate of return.

**Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ annual return (%)**

| Annual return | Rule of 72 estimate | Exact doubling time |
|---|---|---|
| 4% | 18 years | about 17.7 years |
| 6% | 12 years | about 11.9 years |
| 8% | 9 years | about 9.0 years |
| 12% | 6 years | about 6.1 years |

The rule is a shortcut; it is most accurate for rates between about 6% and 10%. For exact figures, use a compound interest calculator, such as the one on Investor.gov.

## Why it works

Doubling depends on **compounding**: earning returns on your past returns as well as on your original money. The higher the rate, the faster growth snowballs.

**Example:** $10,000 growing 8% a year doubles to about $20,000 in 9 years, reaches about $40,000 in 18 years and about $80,000 in 27 years. Starting earlier gives money more doubling periods.

## Using it the other way

You can also find the rate needed to double money in a given time: divide 72 by the number of years. Doubling in 10 years requires about **7.2%** a year.

## It works for costs too

- **Inflation:** at 3% inflation, prices double in about 24 years, so a dollar buys half as much.
- **Debt:** a credit card balance at 24% interest doubles in about 3 years if left unpaid.
- **Fees:** a 1% yearly fee consumes a growing share of returns over decades.

## Caveats

Investment returns aren't steady from year to year; the rule assumes a constant rate, and returns from stocks can be negative for long stretches. It also ignores taxes and fees unless you subtract them from the rate first.

## Key takeaways

- Divide 72 by the annual percentage rate to estimate how many years it takes to double.
- It shows the power of compounding, and of starting early.
- It also shows how fast inflation and high-interest debt grow.
