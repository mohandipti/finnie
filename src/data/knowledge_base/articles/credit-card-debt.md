# Credit Card Debt and Minimum Payments

## Why the minimum payment is costly

The minimum payment is the smallest amount you can pay each month to keep a card in good standing. Interest is charged on any balance you don't pay off, and credit card interest rates are high, so paying only the minimum can keep you in debt for years.

**Example (illustrative calculation):** a $3,000 balance at a 22% APR, with no new purchases:

- paying $60 a month takes about 11 years and about $5,200 in interest;
- paying $150 a month takes just over 2 years and about $770 in interest.

Your statement shows how long paying only the minimum would take, and how much you'd pay in total.

## Two ways to pay down several debts

- **Highest interest rate first (avalanche):** pay the minimum on every card and put extra money toward the one with the highest rate. This usually costs the least interest.
- **Smallest balance first (snowball):** put extra money toward the smallest balance to clear accounts quickly, which some people find motivating.

Either works if you stick with it; both depend on spending less than you earn.

## Consolidation options and their catches

- **Balance transfers:** many cards offer 0% or low rates on transferred balances, but the promotional rate lasts a limited time and there's usually a transfer fee. If you also make new purchases on that card, you lose the grace period on them. Paying more than 60 days late can raise the rate on all balances.
- **Debt consolidation loans:** combine debts into one payment, possibly at a lower rate, but low "teaser" rates may rise, and a longer term can mean paying more overall.
- **Home equity loans:** lower rates, but you could lose your home if you can't repay, and there are closing costs.

Consolidation doesn't help unless the spending that caused the debt changes.

## If you can't pay

Contact your card company right away: many will adjust payments in an emergency. Nonprofit credit counselors can help with a plan. Be wary of debt settlement companies: they aren't allowed to collect fees before settling your debt, and promises to make debt disappear or advice to stop paying are warning signs.

## Key takeaways

- Paying only the minimum can stretch a balance over many years and cost more than the original debt.
- Paying extra toward the highest-rate card usually saves the most interest.
- Balance transfers and consolidation loans have time limits and fees; read the terms first.
