# Gift and Estate Tax Basics

This article describes general US federal rules for 2026. States may have their own estate or inheritance taxes, and estate planning should involve a professional.

## The gift tax

The federal **gift tax** applies to transfers of money or property to another person without receiving full value in return. The **giver**, not the recipient, is responsible for it. Most people never pay it, because of large exclusions.

## The annual exclusion

You can give up to the **annual exclusion** to **each** person every year without any gift tax consequences. For **2026 it is $19,000** per recipient.

**Example:** a couple with three children can each give each child $19,000 in 2026, a total of $114,000, with no gift tax and no gift tax return.

## Gifts that aren't taxable

Generally, these aren't taxable gifts:

- gifts up to the annual exclusion;
- **tuition or medical expenses** you pay **directly** to the school or provider for someone;
- gifts to your **spouse** (if your spouse is a US citizen; gifts to a non-citizen spouse have a separate yearly limit, $194,000 for 2026);
- gifts to political organizations, and gifts to qualifying charities, which are deductible from the gift's value.

## Gifts above the exclusion

If you give one person more than the annual exclusion, you generally file a **gift tax return (Form 709)**, which can be filed electronically. You usually **still owe no tax**: the excess counts against a **lifetime exclusion** shared with the estate tax.

**Example:** you give a friend $25,000 in 2026. The $6,000 above the annual exclusion is reported on Form 709 and reduces your remaining lifetime exclusion, but no tax is due.

## The estate tax

The **estate tax** applies to property left at death. For people who die in 2026, the **basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000**, reduced by taxable gifts made during life. Only estates above that amount owe federal estate tax.

## Income tax isn't affected

Making a gift or leaving an inheritance doesn't ordinarily affect your federal income tax: you can't deduct gifts (except to qualifying charities), and recipients don't owe income tax on gifts. Basis rules do matter: gifts generally keep the giver's basis, while inherited assets generally get a basis equal to their value at death.

## Key takeaways

- In 2026 you can give up to $19,000 per person per year with no gift tax consequences.
- Larger gifts are reported on Form 709 but usually just reduce your lifetime exclusion.
- The 2026 federal estate tax exclusion is $15,000,000, so few estates owe it.
