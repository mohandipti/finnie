# Leveraged and Inverse ETFs and ETNs

Most exchange-traded products (ETPs) are ordinary ETFs that hold stocks or bonds. Some, however, are built for short-term trading and behave very differently.

## Leveraged and inverse ("geared") products

**Leveraged** ETPs aim to deliver a multiple of an index's return, such as two or three times. **Inverse** ETPs aim to deliver the opposite of the index's return, or a multiple of the opposite. FINRA calls these "geared" products.

The key detail: the target is usually for a **single period, often one day**. These products are generally **not designed to be held for long periods**.

## Why holding them is risky

Because they reset every day, returns compound in ways that can surprise investors. Over more than one day, a 2x fund does not necessarily return twice the index.

**Example (illustrative calculation):** an index starts at 100, rises 10% one day and falls 10% the next, ending at 99, a 1% loss. A 2x leveraged fund starting at $100 rises 20% to $120, then falls 20% to $96, a **4% loss**, four times the index's loss rather than twice. In choppy markets these losses can build up even when the index ends up roughly flat.

## Exchange-traded notes (ETNs)

**ETNs** also trade like stocks and track an index or benchmark, but they're very different from ETFs:

- they don't hold any assets; they are **unsecured debt** issued by a bank or other financial institution;
- they're subject to the **credit risk** of that issuer: if it fails, you could lose money even if the index rose;
- they generally don't pay periodic interest; returns depend on the price you sell at, or the payment at maturity;
- some can be **called** (redeemed early) by the issuer, which can cause a loss if the call value is below what you paid.

## Other complex ETPs

Single-stock ETFs, and ETPs tied to commodities, currencies, volatility or narrow indexes, can carry extra risks. Some ETPs that hold commodity futures aren't registered under the Investment Company Act, so they have fewer investor protections. Thinly traded products may have wide bid-ask spreads or trade at a premium or discount to their value.

## Key takeaways

- Leveraged and inverse ETPs target a multiple of an index's return for a short period, usually one day.
- Held for longer, their returns can differ greatly from the multiple you'd expect.
- ETNs are unsecured debt that depends on the issuer's credit, not funds holding assets.
