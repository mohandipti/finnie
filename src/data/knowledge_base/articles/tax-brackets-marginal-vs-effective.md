# Tax Brackets: Marginal vs. Effective Tax Rate

This article describes US federal income tax for tax year 2026 (returns filed in 2027). Brackets are adjusted for inflation every year; check IRS.gov for current figures.

## How brackets work

The US taxes income in layers called **tax brackets**. Each rate applies only to the part of your taxable income that falls inside its bracket. Moving into a higher bracket raises the rate only on the income above that threshold; the rest is still taxed at the lower rates. A raise never leaves you with less after-tax income because of brackets.

## 2026 brackets

| Rate | Single: taxable income over | Married filing jointly: over |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | $0 | $0 |
| 12% | $12,400 | $24,800 |
| 22% | $50,400 | $100,800 |
| 24% | $105,700 | $211,400 |
| 32% | $201,775 | $403,550 |
| 35% | $256,225 | $512,450 |
| 37% | $640,600 | $768,700 |

## Taxable income and the standard deduction

Brackets apply to **taxable income**: income after deductions. Most people take the **standard deduction**, which for 2026 is $16,100 for single filers and $32,200 for married couples filing jointly.

## Marginal vs. effective rate

- **Marginal rate:** the rate on your last (highest) dollar of taxable income, your top bracket.
- **Effective rate:** your total tax divided by your income, the average rate you actually pay.

**Example (single, 2026):** income of $76,100 minus the $16,100 standard deduction leaves $60,000 of taxable income.

- 10% of the first $12,400 = $1,240
- 12% of the next $38,000 (up to $50,400) = $4,560
- 22% of the remaining $9,600 = $2,112

Total tax: $7,912. The marginal rate is 22%, but the effective rate is about 13.2% of taxable income, or about 10.4% of total income.

## Why it matters for investing

The marginal rate is the one that matters when comparing choices, such as a traditional (tax deduction now) vs. Roth (tax-free later) retirement contribution, or a taxable vs. tax-exempt bond. Credits, other deductions and state taxes also affect what a person actually owes.

## Key takeaways

- Each rate applies only to income within its bracket.
- Your marginal rate is your top bracket; your effective rate is your average rate.
- Brackets apply to taxable income, after the standard or itemized deductions.
