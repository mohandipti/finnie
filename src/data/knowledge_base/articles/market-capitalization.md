# Market Capitalization: Large-, Mid- and Small-Cap Stocks

## What market capitalization is

**Market capitalization** ("market cap") is the total market value of a company's shares: the current price of one share multiplied by the number of shares outstanding.

**Example:** a company with 500 million shares trading at $40 has a market cap of $20 billion.

Market cap measures size by what investors collectively say the company is worth, not by revenue or number of employees. It changes every time the share price moves.

## Size categories

Stocks are often grouped by market cap:

- **Large-cap:** the biggest, most established companies. Large, well-known companies with a solid history of growth that generally pay dividends are often called **blue-chip** stocks.
- **Mid-cap:** medium-sized companies, often still growing.
- **Small-cap:** smaller companies, which can grow faster but tend to be more volatile.
- **Microcap:** very small companies. The lowest-priced shares are known as **penny stocks**; these companies may have little or no earnings, don't pay dividends and are highly speculative.

The dollar cut-offs between categories aren't official and vary between index providers and fund companies.

## Why it matters

- **Risk:** smaller companies generally carry more business risk and bigger price swings than large ones.
- **Information:** large companies are followed by many analysts and widely reported on; tiny companies may publish little information, which makes them easier targets for fraud.
- **Indexes:** many indexes, such as the S&P 500, are **market-cap weighted**: the largest companies have the biggest influence on the index's moves.
- **Funds:** stock funds often focus on one size, such as a "large-cap value" or "small-cap growth" fund.

## Share price isn't company size

A $500 stock isn't necessarily a bigger company than a $50 stock; it depends on how many shares each has. Price per share alone also says nothing about whether a stock is expensive. Valuation measures such as the P/E ratio do that.

## Key takeaways

- Market cap = share price × shares outstanding.
- Companies are grouped as large-, mid-, small- and microcap.
- Smaller companies tend to be riskier and more volatile; penny stocks are highly speculative.
