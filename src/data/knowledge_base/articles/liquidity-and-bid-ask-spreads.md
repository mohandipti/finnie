# Liquidity and Bid-Ask Spreads

## What liquidity is

A market has **liquidity** when investors can quickly buy and sell without significantly moving the price. Liquidity falls when buyers and sellers are out of balance or prices are volatile. A security that can't be traded without a big price change, or for which it's hard to find a buyer or seller, is **illiquid**.

Large companies' stocks and popular ETFs are usually very liquid. Small-company stocks, thinly traded ETFs, many bonds and private investments can be much less so.

## The bid and the ask

Every traded security has two prices:

- the **bid**: the highest price a buyer is currently willing to pay;
- the **ask** (or offer): the lowest price a seller is currently willing to accept.

The difference is the **bid-ask spread**. When you buy at market, you generally pay the ask; when you sell, you generally get the bid. The spread is a trading cost, separate from any commission.

**Example:** a stock is quoted $50.00 bid, $50.04 ask, a 4-cent spread. Buying 100 shares and immediately selling them would cost about $4 even if the price didn't move. A thinly traded stock quoted $10.00 bid, $10.25 ask has a spread of 2.5% of the price, a much bigger cost.

## What affects the spread

- **Trading volume:** heavily traded securities usually have narrow spreads; thinly traded ones have wider spreads.
- **Volatility:** spreads tend to widen when markets are turbulent.
- **Market hours:** spreads can be wider near the open, near the close and outside regular trading hours.

## ETFs and spreads

ETF shares trade like stocks, so they also have bid-ask spreads. The spread may be almost zero for some ETFs but much wider for others, and a thinly traded ETF may trade at a premium or discount to the value of its holdings.

## Reducing the cost

A **limit order** lets you set the most you'll pay or the least you'll accept, protecting you from a wide spread. Trading liquid securities during regular hours also helps.

## Key takeaways

- Liquidity is how easily something can be traded without moving its price.
- The bid-ask spread is a hidden trading cost: you buy at the ask and sell at the bid.
- Thinly traded securities have wider spreads; limit orders help control the price you pay.
