# Tax Credits vs. Tax Deductions

Credits and deductions both lower your tax, but in different ways, and a credit is usually worth more than a deduction of the same amount.

## Deductions reduce taxable income

A **deduction** lowers the income your tax is calculated on. How much it saves depends on your **marginal tax rate** (your top bracket).

## Credits reduce the tax itself

A **credit** is an amount subtracted **directly from the tax you owe**. It can lower your tax bill or increase your refund.

**Example:** you're in the 22% bracket.

- A **$1,000 deduction** lowers taxable income by $1,000, saving **$220** of tax (22% × $1,000).
- A **$1,000 credit** lowers your tax by the full **$1,000**.

## Refundable vs. nonrefundable credits

- **Refundable credits** can give you money back even if you owe no tax. The **Earned Income Tax Credit** is refundable: if you qualify, you can claim it even if you don't normally have to file a return.
- **Nonrefundable credits** can reduce your tax to zero, but any unused amount isn't paid to you.

**Example:** you owe $600 of tax and qualify for a $1,000 credit. If it's refundable, your tax drops to zero and you get $400 back. If it's nonrefundable, your tax drops to zero and the extra $400 is lost.

## Common examples

- **Deductions:** the standard deduction or itemized deductions, traditional IRA contributions (for eligible taxpayers), student loan interest, HSA contributions.
- **Credits:** the Earned Income Tax Credit, credits for children and dependents, education credits (American Opportunity and Lifetime Learning) and the Saver's Credit for retirement contributions.

Each has its own eligibility rules and income limits. Tax software asks questions to find the ones you qualify for; on a paper return, credits usually require a separate form.

## Why it matters for investors

Some retirement choices involve both: a traditional IRA contribution may be **deductible**, while a lower-income saver making the same contribution might also qualify for the **Saver's Credit**.

## Key takeaways

- A deduction lowers taxable income; its value depends on your tax bracket.
- A credit lowers your tax dollar for dollar.
- Refundable credits can pay you money even when you owe no tax.
