# What Is a Stock?

## Ownership in a company

A stock (also called a share or equity) represents a small piece of ownership in a company. If a company has 1 million shares and you own 1,000, you own 0.1% of it. Companies sell shares to raise money to grow their business.

## How stock investors can earn money

1. **Price appreciation.** If the company grows and becomes more valuable, its share price may rise, and you can sell for more than you paid.
2. **Dividends.** Some companies pay part of their profits to shareholders, usually quarterly. Not all companies pay dividends; many growth companies reinvest profits instead.

## Why stock prices move

A share price reflects what buyers and sellers agree a share is worth at a given moment. Prices react to company earnings, expectations about future growth, interest rates, the economy and investor sentiment. In the short term, prices can move sharply for reasons that have little to do with the company's long-term value.

## Risks of owning individual stocks

- **Company risk.** A single company can perform badly or even go bankrupt, and its shares can lose most or all of their value.
- **Volatility.** Individual stocks often swing more in price than broad funds.
- **Concentration.** Holding only a few stocks ties your results to a few businesses.

Many investors manage these risks by owning many companies at once, for example through index funds or ETFs.

## Common stock terms

- **Ticker symbol:** a short code that identifies a stock, such as AAPL for Apple.
- **Market capitalization:** share price × number of shares; a measure of company size.
- **Exchange:** a marketplace where shares are traded, such as the NYSE or Nasdaq.

## Key takeaways

- A stock is partial ownership of a company.
- Returns come from price changes and, sometimes, dividends.
- Individual stocks carry company-specific risk that diversification can reduce.
