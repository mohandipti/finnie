# Compound Interest: How Money Grows Over Time

## What compound interest is

Compound interest is interest earned on both your original money (the principal) and on the interest it has already earned. Simple interest, by contrast, is paid only on the principal. Over long periods, compounding makes a large difference because each year's growth starts from a bigger base.

## A simple example

Suppose you invest $1,000 and it grows by 7% a year:

- After 1 year: $1,070
- After 10 years: about $1,967
- After 30 years: about $7,612

In the first year you earn $70. In year 30 you earn roughly $500, even though you never added money, because you are earning growth on all the growth from earlier years.

## The three things that drive compounding

1. **Rate of return.** A higher rate grows money faster, but higher expected returns usually come with more risk.
2. **Time.** The longer money compounds, the stronger the effect. Starting early often matters more than the amount you start with.
3. **Regular contributions.** Adding money regularly gives each new contribution its own time to compound.

## The Rule of 72

A quick way to estimate how long it takes money to double: divide 72 by the annual rate of return. At 7%, money doubles in about 72 ÷ 7 ≈ 10 years. At 4%, it takes about 18 years.

## Compounding works against you too

Debt compounds in the same way. Credit card balances with high interest rates can grow quickly if only minimum payments are made, which is why paying down high-interest debt is often discussed alongside investing.

## Key takeaways

- Compounding means earning returns on previous returns.
- Time is one of the most powerful factors.
- Returns on investments are not guaranteed; the examples above assume a steady rate, which real markets do not provide.
