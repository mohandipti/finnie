# Employer 401(k) Matching and Vesting

## The employer match

Many employers add money to your 401(k) when you contribute, called a **matching contribution**. A common design matches a percentage of what you put in, up to a limit set by the plan.

**Example:** a plan matches 50% of your contributions up to 6% of your salary. On a $60,000 salary, contributing 6% ($3,600 a year) earns a $1,800 match. Contributing only 3% would earn just $900, leaving $900 of potential match unused each year.

Your plan's **Summary Plan Description (SPD)**, which the administrator must give you within 90 days of joining, explains the match formula. Employer matches don't count toward your own yearly contribution limit.

## Vesting: when the match becomes yours

**Vesting** is when you gain ownership of money in the plan.

- Your **own contributions**, and the earnings on them, are always **100% vested** immediately.
- **Employer contributions** may vest over time, according to the plan's schedule.

For employer matches in a traditional 401(k), plans generally use one of two schedules:

- **Cliff vesting:** 0% until you complete **3 years** of service, then 100%.
- **Graded vesting:** at least **20% after 2 years**, increasing each year to **100% after 6 years**.

Some plans vest faster or immediately. In **safe harbor 401(k)** plans, required employer contributions are always 100% vested.

## Why it matters when changing jobs

If you leave before you're fully vested, you forfeit the unvested part of the employer's contributions.

**Example:** you've received $9,000 in matches over 4 years in a plan with a graded schedule where you're 60% vested. If you leave now, you keep $5,400 of the match plus all of your own contributions; the other $3,600 goes back to the plan. If you return within five years, a plan generally must preserve the service credit you'd earned.

## Key takeaways

- An employer match is extra money for retirement; contributing enough to get the full match is one of the most valuable steps in a 401(k).
- Your own contributions are always yours; employer contributions may vest over 3 to 6 years.
- Check your plan's vesting schedule before changing jobs.
