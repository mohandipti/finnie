# Variable Annuities: What to Know

## What a variable annuity is

A **variable annuity** is a contract between you and an **insurance company** that works as an investment account. The money is invested in options similar to mutual funds, so its value goes up and down with the markets. It also includes **insurance features**, such as turning the account into a stream of income payments and a **death benefit** for beneficiaries.

## Potential benefits

- **Tax-deferred growth:** investment gains aren't taxed until withdrawn.
- **Income options:** the account can be converted into periodic payments, potentially for life.
- **Death benefit:** for example, a beneficiary may receive the greater of the account value or the total purchase payments minus prior withdrawals.
- **Optional living benefits** (for an extra fee), such as a guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefit that allows withdrawals up to a set percentage each year even if the account value falls.

## Costs and drawbacks

- **Fees** can be significant: insurance charges, investment fund expenses, and extra charges for optional features. Fees taken from payments mean less of your money is invested.
- **Surrender charges:** withdrawing money during the early years of the contract usually triggers a charge, which typically declines over several years. Exchanging one annuity for another can start a new surrender period.
- **Bonus credits** sometimes come with higher fees or longer surrender periods that can outweigh the bonus.
- **Limited access:** once income payments start, you generally can't withdraw from the account.
- **Insurer strength:** guarantees depend on the financial strength of the insurance company.
- Withdrawals of earnings are taxed as ordinary income, and withdrawals before age 59½ may face an additional tax penalty.

## The free-look period

Most contracts allow you to cancel within a short **free-look period** (usually 10 to 30 days) after receiving the contract, without a surrender charge.

## Key takeaways

- Variable annuities combine market investments with insurance features and tax deferral.
- Fees, surrender charges and limited access are the main trade-offs.
- Guarantees are only as strong as the insurance company behind them.
