# Money Market Funds

## What a money market fund is

A money market fund is a type of mutual fund that invests in high-quality, short-term debt, such as Treasury bills and commercial paper. Investors often use them to hold cash they may need soon, or as the cash position in a brokerage account.

A money market **fund** is not the same as a money market **deposit account** at a bank. A deposit account is FDIC-insured; a money market fund is an investment and **is not guaranteed by the FDIC**, so you could lose money.

## Types of money market funds

- **Government funds** invest 99.5% or more of their assets in cash, government securities and repurchase agreements backed by them.
- **Prime funds** invest mainly in taxable short-term corporate and bank debt, such as commercial paper and CDs.
- **Tax-exempt (municipal) funds** generally invest 80% or more in municipal securities whose interest is exempt from federal and/or state income tax.

## The $1.00 share price

Most money market funds, including those for retail investors and government funds, seek to keep a **stable net asset value (NAV) of $1.00 per share**, using special pricing methods. Institutional prime and institutional tax-exempt funds must instead let their NAV "float" like other mutual funds.

A stable price is a goal, not a guarantee. If a stable-NAV fund's value per share moves more than half a cent from $1.00, it must re-price its shares, known as **"breaking the buck"**. This is rare, but it happened in 2008, when the Reserve Primary Fund fell below $1.00 during the financial crisis.

## Liquidity fees

When many investors sell at once, a fund may charge a **liquidity fee** on withdrawals to protect remaining shareholders. All money market funds may charge one; institutional prime and tax-exempt funds generally must when daily net redemptions exceed 5% of assets.

**Example:** $10,000 in a government money market fund earns interest close to short-term Treasury rates and usually holds steady at $1.00 a share, but it isn't insured the way a bank deposit is.

## Key takeaways

- Money market funds invest in short-term debt and aim for a stable $1.00 share price.
- They are investments, not FDIC-insured bank deposits.
- Government funds hold the safest assets; prime funds hold corporate debt for slightly higher yields.
