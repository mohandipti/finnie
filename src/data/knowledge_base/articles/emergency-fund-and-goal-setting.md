# Emergency Funds and Setting Financial Goals

## The emergency fund comes first

An emergency fund is money set aside for unexpected expenses, such as a job loss, medical bill or car repair. A common guideline is three to six months of essential living expenses, kept somewhere safe and easy to access, such as a high-yield savings account.

Without one, an emergency may force you to sell investments at a bad time or take on high-interest debt. An emergency fund can start small, for example $25 per paycheck, and automatic transfers make saving consistent.

Paying off high-interest debt, such as credit card balances, is also commonly prioritized before investing: as Investor.gov notes, no investment strategy consistently pays off as well as, or with less risk than, eliminating high-interest debt.

## Making goals specific

Vague goals ("save more") are hard to plan for. A useful goal has:

- **A target amount:** for example, $60,000.
- **A deadline:** for example, in 5 years.
- **A purpose:** for example, a house down payment.

## Time horizon shapes the plan

- **Short-term goals (under about 3 years):** money is usually kept in stable options like savings accounts or short-term bonds, because there is little time to recover from a market drop.
- **Long-term goals (10+ years), such as retirement:** there is more time to ride out market swings, so growth investments like stocks are commonly part of the mix.

## Working backwards to a monthly amount

Once you know the target and deadline, you can estimate the monthly saving needed. Saving $60,000 in 5 years with no growth requires $1,000 a month. With investment growth, the required amount is lower, but growth is never guaranteed, and higher assumed returns come with more risk.

## The three levers

If a goal looks out of reach, there are three levers to adjust:

1. **Time:** extend the deadline.
2. **Contributions:** save more each month.
3. **Risk:** accept more investment risk for higher expected (but uncertain) growth.

## Key takeaways

- Build an emergency fund before investing for other goals.
- Good goals have an amount, a deadline and a purpose.
- Time horizon determines how much risk a goal can reasonably take.
