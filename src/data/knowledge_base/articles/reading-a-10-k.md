# How to Read a Company's 10-K

## What a 10-K is

Most U.S. public companies must file an annual report with the SEC on **Form 10-K**. It gives a detailed picture of the company's **business**, the **risks** it faces, and its **operating and financial results** for the year. The company writes the 10-K; the SEC sets the disclosure rules but does not vouch for the company or its investment merits.

## Where to find it

Every 10-K filed with the SEC is free to read on the SEC's **EDGAR** database. Many companies also post them on their investor-relations websites.

## The most useful sections for beginners

A 10-K follows a standard layout, which makes companies easier to compare:

- **Item 1, Business:** what the company does, its products and services, markets and competition. A good place to start.
- **Item 1A, Risk Factors:** the most significant risks to the company and its stock, generally listed in order of importance.
- **Item 7, Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A):** management's explanation of the year's results, trends, liquidity and key uncertainties.
- **Item 8, Financial Statements:** the audited income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement, plus notes. The **auditor's report** is a key part of this section.

Later parts cover governance, executive pay and ownership (often included from the company's proxy statement), and a list of exhibits.

## 10-K vs. annual report to shareholders

Companies also send shareholders an **annual report**, sometimes a glossy publication. The two overlap, but the 10-K usually contains **more detail**, and some companies simply send the 10-K as their annual report.

## Tips for reading one

- Start with Business and Risk Factors to understand what could go wrong.
- Read the MD&A to see how management explains changes in results.
- Compare several years of filings to spot trends.

## Key takeaways

- The 10-K is a company's detailed annual disclosure, free on EDGAR.
- Business, Risk Factors, MD&A and the audited financial statements are the core sections.
- It is written by the company, so read it critically.
