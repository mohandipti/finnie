# Rebalancing Your Portfolio

## What rebalancing is

**Rebalancing** is bringing your portfolio back to your original **asset allocation**: the mix of stocks, bonds and cash you chose for your goals and risk tolerance. Over time, some investments grow faster than others, so the mix drifts. Rebalancing keeps your portfolio from overemphasizing one asset category and taking on more (or less) risk than you intended.

**Example (from Investor.gov):** you decide stocks should be 60% of your portfolio. After a stock market rise, they've grown to 80%. To get back to 60%, you can sell some stocks or buy more of the under-weighted categories, such as bonds.

## Three ways to rebalance

1. **Sell and buy:** sell some of the over-weighted category and use the money to buy the under-weighted one.
2. **Buy only:** add new money to the under-weighted category.
3. **Redirect contributions:** if you contribute regularly, send more of each contribution to the under-weighted category until the mix is back on target.

Also check the investments **within** each category, for example if one stock has grown to dominate your stock holdings.

## When to rebalance

- **On a schedule:** many financial experts suggest every **six or twelve months**. The calendar acts as a reminder.
- **By thresholds:** only when an asset class moves more than a set percentage from its target, such as 5 percentage points. This responds to the market rather than the date.

## Buy low, sell high

Rebalancing means trimming what has done well and adding to what has lagged. That's emotionally hard but useful: it forces you to **buy low and sell high**, rather than chasing whatever is hot. Experienced investors usually don't change their allocation because one category is doing well; they rebalance instead.

## Costs and taxes

Before rebalancing, consider **transaction fees and taxes**. Selling winners in a taxable account can create capital gains. Rebalancing inside a 401(k) or IRA, or using new contributions instead of selling, can reduce these costs. Target date and other lifecycle funds rebalance automatically.

## Key takeaways

- Rebalancing restores your chosen mix of stocks, bonds and cash as markets move.
- Rebalance on a schedule, such as yearly, or when the mix drifts past a set threshold.
- Watch for taxes and fees; using new contributions or tax-advantaged accounts can help.
