# The Saver's Credit for Retirement Contributions

This article describes the general US federal rules. Income limits change every year; check IRS.gov for the current table.

## What it is

The **Saver's Credit** (officially the retirement savings contributions credit) is a tax credit for low- and moderate-income workers who save for retirement. Because it's a **credit**, it reduces your tax dollar for dollar, on top of any tax benefit the contribution itself gives you.

## Who is eligible

You're eligible if you are:

- **age 18 or older**;
- **not claimed as a dependent** on someone else's return; and
- **not a student** (you were a full-time student during any part of 5 months of the year).

Your income must also be below a limit that depends on your filing status. For 2026, the income limit for married couples filing jointly is **$80,500**, with lower limits for other filers.

## Which contributions count

- contributions to a traditional or Roth **IRA**;
- elective deferrals to a **401(k), 403(b), governmental 457(b), SARSEP or SIMPLE** plan;
- voluntary after-tax contributions to a qualified plan, including the federal Thrift Savings Plan;
- contributions to an **ABLE account** by its beneficiary.

## How much

The credit is **50%, 20% or 10%** of up to **$2,000** of contributions (**$4,000** for married couples filing jointly), depending on your adjusted gross income. The lower your income, the higher the rate. The maximum credit is **$1,000** ($2,000 for married couples filing jointly).

**Example:** a single worker with income low enough for the 50% rate contributes $2,000 to a Roth IRA. They can get a $1,000 credit, effectively getting half of the contribution back through lower taxes.

The credit is nonrefundable: it can reduce your tax to zero but won't pay you more than you owe. Recent withdrawals from retirement accounts can reduce the contributions that count.

## How to claim

Claim it on **Form 8880** with your tax return; tax software asks about it.

## Key takeaways

- The Saver's Credit rewards low- and moderate-income workers for retirement contributions.
- It's worth 50%, 20% or 10% of up to $2,000 of contributions ($4,000 for joint filers).
- You must be 18 or older, not a dependent and not a full-time student.
