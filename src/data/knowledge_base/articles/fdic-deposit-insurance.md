# FDIC Deposit Insurance

## What it is

The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) insures deposits at FDIC-insured banks in case the bank fails. Since the FDIC was founded in 1933, no depositor has lost a penny of FDIC-insured funds. The insurance fund is backed by the full faith and credit of the US government. Credit unions have similar coverage through the NCUA.

## What is covered

Deposits at FDIC-insured banks in:

- checking and savings accounts;
- money market deposit accounts (MMDAs);
- certificates of deposit (CDs);
- cashier's checks, money orders and other official items issued by the bank.

## What is not covered

FDIC insurance covers deposits only. It does **not** cover stocks, bonds, mutual funds (including money market funds), annuities, life insurance, safe deposit boxes, Treasury securities, municipal securities or crypto assets, even when they are bought through an FDIC-insured bank.

## How much is covered

Coverage is **$250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, for each account ownership category**. Ownership categories include single accounts, joint accounts, certain retirement accounts such as IRAs, trust accounts and business accounts. All your deposits in the same category at the same bank are added together.

**Examples:**

- A checking account and a savings account in your name alone at one bank are both "single" accounts, so together they're insured up to $250,000.
- An IRA at the same bank is a different category, so it's insured separately for up to another $250,000.
- A single account at a second bank is insured separately for up to $250,000 at that bank.

The FDIC's Electronic Deposit Insurance Estimator (EDIE) calculates coverage for a specific set of accounts, and its BankFind tool confirms whether a bank is FDIC-insured.

## When a bank fails

Bank failures are unlikely, but they happen. When they do, the FDIC acts quickly so that access to insured deposits isn't interrupted, typically by moving them to another bank or paying depositors directly.

## Key takeaways

- FDIC insurance protects bank deposits, not investments, up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category.
- Spreading money across ownership categories or banks can increase total coverage.
- Mutual funds, stocks, bonds and crypto bought through a bank are not FDIC-insured.
