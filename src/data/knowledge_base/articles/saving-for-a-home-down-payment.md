# Saving for a Home: Down Payments, Closing Costs and Mortgage Insurance

## How much down payment

In most cases, you need a down payment of **at least 3%** of the home price, and many loan types and lenders require **5% or more**. A bigger down payment can save money:

- you can often save if you put down **at least 10%**;
- you save the most with **at least 20%**, which usually avoids mortgage insurance.

Lenders typically price loans in **5% steps** of down payment, so there's usually no benefit to putting down "almost" the next level; 8% may be priced like 5%.

## Don't forget closing costs

On top of the down payment, buying a home involves **closing costs**: lender fees, appraisal, title insurance and other charges. They typically run **2–5% of the purchase price**, depending on the home, loan, lender and location.

## Keep an emergency cushion

The CFPB suggests working out your cash for closing as your savings, minus an emergency cushion of at least **three to six months of expenses**, so a new home doesn't leave you with no safety net.

**Example:** you have $60,000 saved and monthly expenses of $4,000. Keeping $18,000 (4.5 months) as a cushion leaves $42,000. For a $300,000 home, closing costs of about 3% ($9,000) leave $33,000, about 11% of the price, for the down payment.

## Mortgage insurance

Borrowers putting down **less than 20%** usually pay for **mortgage insurance**. It is also required on FHA and USDA loans.

- It **protects the lender, not you**, if you fall behind on payments.
- **Private mortgage insurance (PMI)** on conventional loans varies with your down payment and credit score, and is usually paid monthly. Under certain circumstances, you can cancel it.
- **FHA** mortgage insurance costs the same regardless of credit score and includes an upfront and an annual premium.

"No PMI" offers aren't free: they often come with a higher interest rate or a second mortgage.

## Where to keep the savings

Money for a home purchase in the next few years is usually kept somewhere stable, such as a high-yield savings account, money market account or CDs, because stock prices can fall right when you need the money.

## Key takeaways

- Down payments start around 3–5%; 20% usually avoids mortgage insurance.
- Budget another 2–5% of the price for closing costs, and keep an emergency cushion.
- Mortgage insurance protects the lender and adds to your monthly cost.
