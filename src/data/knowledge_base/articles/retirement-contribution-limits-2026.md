# Retirement Account Contribution Limits for 2026

This article lists the US federal limits the IRS announced for 2026 (News Release IR-2025-111). Limits are adjusted for inflation most years; check IRS.gov for other years.

## Workplace plans: 401(k), 403(b), 457 and TSP

| | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Employee contribution limit | $23,500 | $24,500 |
| Extra catch-up, age 50 and over | $7,500 | $8,000 |
| Extra catch-up, ages 60 to 63 | $11,250 | $11,250 |

The employee limit covers your own contributions, traditional and Roth combined. Employer matching contributions don't count toward it.

**Example:** a 52-year-old can contribute up to $24,500 + $8,000 = $32,500 to their 401(k) in 2026. A 61-year-old can contribute up to $24,500 + $11,250 = $35,750.

## IRAs

| | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Contribution limit (traditional and Roth combined) | $7,000 | $7,500 |
| Extra catch-up, age 50 and over | $1,000 | $1,100 |

## Roth IRA income limits

The amount you can contribute to a Roth IRA is reduced gradually, and then eliminated, as income rises through a **phase-out range** (based on modified adjusted gross income). For 2026:

- single and head of household: $153,000 to $168,000;
- married filing jointly: $242,000 to $252,000.

## Deducting traditional IRA contributions

Anyone with earned income can contribute to a traditional IRA, but if you (or your spouse) are covered by a workplace retirement plan, the **tax deduction** phases out at higher incomes. For 2026:

- single, covered by a workplace plan: $81,000 to $91,000;
- married filing jointly, contributor covered by a workplace plan: $129,000 to $149,000;
- not covered, but married to someone who is: $242,000 to $252,000.

Above these ranges, contributions are still allowed but aren't deductible.

## Key takeaways

- For 2026, the 401(k) employee limit is $24,500 and the IRA limit is $7,500.
- People aged 50 and over can make extra catch-up contributions, with a higher 401(k) catch-up at ages 60 to 63.
- Income limits apply to Roth IRA contributions and to deducting traditional IRA contributions.
