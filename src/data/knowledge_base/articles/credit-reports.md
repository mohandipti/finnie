# Credit Reports: Getting Yours Free and Fixing Errors

## What a credit report is

A credit report is a summary of your personal credit history. It includes identifying information, such as your name and address, and details of your credit accounts: what you owe, your payment history, and records such as bankruptcies. Lenders, landlords and others use it, and credit scores are calculated from it.

The three nationwide credit bureaus that keep these reports are **Equifax, Experian and TransUnion**. Each keeps its own report, so the three may differ.

## Getting your reports for free

Federal law entitles you to a free report from each of the three bureaus every 12 months, and the bureaus have **permanently** extended a program that lets you check your report from each of them **once a week for free**.

The official source is **AnnualCreditReport.com** (or its toll-free number or mail form). The FTC advises ordering there rather than contacting each bureau separately.

**Example:** you could check one bureau's report at the start of each month, rotating through Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, to keep an eye on your credit all year at no cost.

## Watch out for imposters

Other websites may claim to offer free credit reports, free credit scores or free monitoring, or use misspellings of the official address. Official sources won't email you asking for your Social Security number; if you get an unsolicited message like that, don't reply or click its links.

## Fixing errors

Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, the credit bureaus must let you dispute mistakes. To fix an error, contact both the credit bureau and the business that supplied the wrong information, explain what is wrong and include copies of documents that support your case.

## Why check regularly

Checking your reports regularly helps you catch errors that could lower your credit scores, and spot signs of identity theft, such as accounts you didn't open.

## Key takeaways

- Equifax, Experian and TransUnion each keep a credit report on you.
- You can check each report for free every week at AnnualCreditReport.com.
- Dispute errors with both the credit bureau and the company that reported the information.
