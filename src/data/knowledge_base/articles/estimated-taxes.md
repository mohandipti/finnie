# Estimated Tax Payments

This article describes general US federal rules for individuals; states may have their own estimated tax rules.

## What estimated taxes are

The US tax system is pay-as-you-go: tax is due as you earn income during the year. Employers withhold tax from paychecks, but income such as **self-employment earnings, interest, dividends, capital gains, rent and alimony** often has no withholding. Estimated tax payments cover the tax on that income.

## Who has to pay

You generally must make estimated payments for the year if both apply:

1. you expect to owe **at least $1,000** in tax after subtracting withholding and refundable credits; and
2. your withholding and credits will be less than the smaller of:
   - **90%** of the tax on this year's return, or
   - **100%** of the tax on last year's return (**110%** if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000, or $75,000 if married filing separately).

These "safe harbor" rules mean that paying at least last year's tax, through withholding or estimated payments, generally avoids a penalty even if you owe more when you file.

## When to pay

| Income earned | Payment due |
|---|---|
| January 1 – March 31 | April 15 |
| April 1 – May 31 | June 15 |
| June 1 – August 31 | September 15 |
| September 1 – December 31 | January 15 of the next year |

If a due date falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day. You can pay online through your IRS account, Direct Pay, by phone or with the IRS app.

**Example:** a freelancer with no withholding expects about $8,000 of federal tax this year. Paying roughly $2,000 by each due date keeps them on track and avoids a penalty for paying too late.

## The penalty

If you don't pay enough by each due date, you may owe a **penalty**, even if you get a refund when you file. It is charged on the amount underpaid for each period.

## Alternatives

If you also have a job, you can increase withholding from your paycheck (by giving your employer a new Form W-4) instead of making estimated payments. Withholding counts as paid evenly through the year, regardless of when it happens.

## Key takeaways

- Estimated taxes cover income without withholding, such as self-employment and investment income.
- Pay if you'll owe $1,000 or more and withholding won't meet the 90%/100% (or 110%) safe harbors.
- Payments are due in April, June, September and January.
