# Fixed and Indexed Annuities

## What an annuity is

An **annuity** is a contract with an insurance company designed for retirement and other long-term goals. You pay a lump sum or a series of payments; in return, the insurer agrees to make income payments to you, starting now or later, or you may withdraw the money as a lump sum.

- With an **immediate annuity**, you pay once and income usually starts within a year.
- With a **deferred annuity**, your money grows **tax-deferred** until you take income or withdrawals.

Every annuity depends on the **insurance company's financial strength**: if the insurer runs into financial trouble, it may not be able to pay.

## Types of deferred annuities, from lower to higher risk

- **Fixed annuities** guarantee a **minimum interest rate** while your money grows. The insurer may pay more, but only the minimum is guaranteed.
- **Fixed indexed annuities** credit interest based partly on an index such as the S&P 500, at the end of a term such as one year. You don't know the rate in advance, but it's guaranteed **never to be less than zero**, even if the market falls. Gains are usually limited by the contract.
- **Registered index-linked annuities (RILAs)** also follow an index, but **you can lose money**: the credited rate can be negative, usually within limits.
- **Variable annuities** invest in funds you choose, so their value rises and falls with the markets.

**Example:** in a year the S&P 500 drops 15%, a fixed indexed annuity credits 0%, so the value doesn't fall, while a RILA might credit a loss. In a year the index rises 20%, the fixed indexed annuity's credit might be limited to a much smaller gain.

## Costs and restrictions

- **Surrender charges:** many annuities charge a fee if you withdraw money within a certain number of years; these usually decline over time.
- **Contract adjustments:** withdrawing before a term ends may forfeit interest, and a market value adjustment can reduce your value.
- **Taxes:** withdrawals of earnings are taxed as ordinary income, and taking money out **before age 59½** may add a **10% IRS penalty**.
- **Implicit costs:** limits on index gains act as a hidden cost of the downside protection.

## Before buying

Annuities are complex and often sold on commission. Read the contract, understand the surrender period and limits on gains, and check the insurer's financial strength. Most states give a short "free look" period to cancel a new contract.

## Key takeaways

- Annuities are insurance contracts that can provide tax-deferred growth and guaranteed income.
- Fixed annuities guarantee a minimum rate; fixed indexed annuities can't credit less than zero; RILAs and variable annuities can lose value.
- Surrender charges, limits on gains and early-withdrawal penalties make them long-term commitments.
