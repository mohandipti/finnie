# APR vs. APY: Comparing Interest Rates

## Two different measures

- **APR (annual percentage rate)** is used for **borrowing**: loans and credit cards. It is the yearly cost of credit: the interest rate plus certain additional fees, such as origination charges.
- **APY (annual percentage yield)** is used for **saving**: savings accounts, money market deposit accounts and CDs. It is the total interest you earn in a year, including the effect of **compounding** (earning interest on interest).

## APR: the cost of borrowing

A loan's interest rate is what you pay the lender for borrowing the principal. The APR adds the lender's fees, so it is usually the better measure of a loan's total cost. Under the Truth in Lending Act, lenders must disclose the APR before you finalize a loan, along with the finance charge (the cost of credit in dollars) and the total of payments.

When comparing loans, compare APR with APR, not one lender's APR with another's interest rate. In general, a higher credit score leads to lower rates, but lenders aren't required to offer you their best rate, so shopping around helps.

## APY: what savings earn

Under the Truth in Savings Act, banks must disclose the APY on deposit accounts, along with the interest rate, minimum balance requirements and fees. Because APY includes compounding, it lets you compare accounts that compound interest differently.

**Example:** a savings account with a 5.00% interest rate compounded monthly has an APY of about 5.12%. Compounded daily, the APY is about 5.13%. On $10,000 left for a year, monthly compounding earns about $512 instead of the $500 simple interest would pay.

APY doesn't reflect penalties, such as an early withdrawal penalty on a CD, so check those separately.

## Which to look for

| You are... | Compare | Better choice |
|---|---|---|
| Borrowing | APR | Lower |
| Saving | APY | Higher |

## Key takeaways

- APR is the yearly cost of borrowing, including some fees; lower is better.
- APY is the yearly return on savings, including compounding; higher is better.
- Compare like with like: APR to APR, APY to APY.
