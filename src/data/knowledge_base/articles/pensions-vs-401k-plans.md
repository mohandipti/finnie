# Pensions vs. 401(k) Plans: Defined Benefit and Defined Contribution

Employer retirement plans come in two main types, and they put the investment risk on different people.

## Defined benefit plans (pensions)

A **defined benefit plan**, funded by the employer, **promises a specific monthly benefit at retirement**. The benefit is often calculated from your salary, age and years of service.

**Example:** a pension might pay 1.5% of your average final salary for each year worked. After 30 years with an average final salary of $70,000, that's 45% × $70,000 = $31,500 a year, for life.

- The **employer** invests the money and bears the investment risk.
- The federal government, through the **Pension Benefit Guaranty Corporation (PBGC)**, insures most private defined benefit plans, up to legal limits, if the plan can't pay.
- If you leave the job, your vested benefit generally stays with the plan until you claim it at retirement.

## Defined contribution plans (401(k)s and similar)

A **defined contribution plan** does **not promise a specific benefit**. You (and often your employer) contribute to an individual account, and what you have at retirement depends on how much went in and how the investments performed. 401(k), 403(b) and 457 plans are examples.

- **You** choose the investments, usually from a menu, and bear the investment risk.
- Defined contribution plans are **not insured** by the PBGC.
- If you leave the job, you can usually move your vested balance to an IRA or a new employer's plan. Cashing out triggers taxes and possibly penalties.

There are several types of 401(k): traditional, safe harbor, SIMPLE and automatic enrollment plans, which differ mainly in how employer contributions and vesting work.

## Comparing them

| | Defined benefit (pension) | Defined contribution (401(k)) |
|---|---|---|
| Benefit | Set by formula | Depends on contributions and returns |
| Investment risk | Employer | Employee |
| Federal insurance | PBGC, for most private plans | None |
| Portability | Stays with the plan | Can roll over to an IRA |

## Know your plan

Your plan's **Summary Plan Description** explains how benefits are calculated, vesting and how to claim. Pensions have become less common in private companies, so many workers rely mainly on a 401(k), Social Security and their own savings.

## Key takeaways

- A pension promises a set income; a 401(k) builds an account whose value depends on savings and returns.
- Pension investment risk is the employer's; 401(k) risk is yours.
- Most private pensions are insured by the PBGC; 401(k)s are not.
