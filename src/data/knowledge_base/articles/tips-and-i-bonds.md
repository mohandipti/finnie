# Inflation-Protected Bonds: TIPS and I Bonds

Both Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) and Series I savings bonds (I bonds) protect against inflation, which erodes the value of ordinary fixed-rate bonds. They work quite differently.

## TIPS

TIPS are marketable Treasury securities whose **principal adjusts with inflation**, measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI-U).

- Issued in 5-, 10- and 30-year terms, at auction through TreasuryDirect or through banks and brokers; minimum $100.
- The interest rate is fixed at auction and never changes, but it is paid every six months on the **adjusted principal**, so payments rise with inflation.
- At maturity you receive the inflation-adjusted principal or the original principal, **whichever is larger**.
- They can be sold before maturity, at market prices that can be below what you paid.

**Example:** you buy $10,000 of TIPS with a 2% rate. If inflation is 3% in a year, the principal grows to about $10,300, and the 2% interest is paid on $10,300.

Tax note: both the interest and the yearly increases in principal are federally taxable in the year they happen, even though the principal increase isn't paid until maturity. They're exempt from state and local income taxes.

## I bonds

I bonds are **non-marketable savings bonds**: registered to you and can't be sold to anyone else.

- Bought only electronically at TreasuryDirect, from $25, up to **$10,000 per Social Security number per calendar year**.
- The rate combines a **fixed rate**, which never changes, with an **inflation rate** reset every six months (announced in May and November).
- Interest is added to the bond and paid when you cash it in; the bond earns interest for up to 30 years.
- You can cash in after **12 months**, but if you do so before 5 years, you lose the **last 3 months of interest**.
- Federal tax on the interest can be deferred until you cash in the bond; it's exempt from state and local taxes.

## Comparing them

| | TIPS | I bonds |
|---|---|---|
| Can be sold to others | Yes | No |
| Yearly purchase limit | Very high | $10,000 per person |
| Interest paid | Every six months | When cashed in |
| Federal tax | Each year | Can be deferred |
| Price can fall | Yes, if sold early | No |

## Key takeaways

- TIPS adjust their principal for inflation and pay interest twice a year; they can be traded.
- I bonds combine a fixed rate with an inflation rate, are limited to $10,000 a year and can't be sold.
- Cashing I bonds in before 5 years costs the last 3 months of interest.
