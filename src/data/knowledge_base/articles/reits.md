# Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs)

## What a REIT is

A **real estate investment trust (REIT)** is a company that owns, and usually operates, income-producing real estate or related assets: office buildings, shopping malls, apartments, hotels, self-storage facilities, warehouses, or mortgages and loans. REITs let individuals invest in large-scale real estate without buying property themselves.

Most REITs pay out all of their taxable income to shareholders as dividends, which is why some offer higher dividend yields than other investments.

## Publicly traded vs. non-traded REITs

This is one of the most important distinctions:

- **Publicly traded REITs** are listed on a stock exchange. You can buy and sell shares through a broker like any stock, and their price is easy to check. REIT mutual funds and ETFs hold many REITs at once.
- **Non-traded REITs** are registered with the SEC but don't trade on an exchange, and are usually sold by a broker or financial adviser.

## Risks of non-traded REITs

- **Illiquid:** they generally can't be sold readily, so you may not get your money out when you need it.
- **Unclear value:** they typically don't estimate a value per share until 18 months after their offering closes, which may be years after you invest.
- **High up-front fees:** sales commissions and offering costs usually total about **9 to 10%** of the investment, which significantly lowers its value.
- **Distributions from borrowed money:** they often pay distributions larger than their income from operations, using offering proceeds or borrowing, which reduces the value of the shares.
- **Conflicts of interest:** they usually have an external manager whose fees may depend on how much property is bought, not on shareholder returns.

**Example:** $10,000 invested in a non-traded REIT with 10% up-front costs leaves roughly $9,000 actually invested in real estate on day one.

## Taxes

REIT dividends are generally taxed as **ordinary income**, not at the lower rates for qualified dividends. Holding REITs in a tax-advantaged account such as an IRA avoids this, although it can't change other risks.

## Avoiding fraud

Be wary of anyone selling REITs that aren't registered with the SEC. You can verify registration of both traded and non-traded REITs on the SEC's EDGAR database.

## Key takeaways

- REITs let you invest in income-producing real estate and usually pay high dividends.
- Publicly traded REITs are liquid; non-traded REITs are hard to sell, hard to value and expensive.
- REIT dividends are generally taxed as ordinary income.
