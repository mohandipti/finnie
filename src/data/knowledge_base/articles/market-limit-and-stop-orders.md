# Market, Limit and Stop Orders

## Why order types matter

When buying or selling a stock or ETF through a broker, the **order type** tells the broker how to execute the trade. The main trade-off is between **certainty of execution** (the trade happens) and **certainty of price** (the price you get).

## Market order

A **market order** buys or sells at the **best available price** right now. It is generally executed immediately, but the price is **not guaranteed**.

The price you get can differ from the last-traded price you saw, especially for fast-moving or thinly traded stocks. Parts of a large market order may even fill at different prices.

## Limit order

A **limit order** buys or sells at a **specific price or better**:

- A **buy limit** executes only at the limit price or lower.
- A **sell limit** executes only at the limit price or higher.

**Example:** a buy limit at $10 for a stock trading at $10.50 only fills if the price drops to $10 or below.

A limit order protects against paying more (or receiving less) than you intended, but it is **not guaranteed to execute**: if the price never reaches your limit, nothing happens.

## Stop order (stop-loss)

A **stop order** becomes a **market order** once the stock reaches a set price, called the **stop price**. Investors often place a **sell stop** below the current price to limit a loss or protect a gain on shares they own.

Because a triggered stop becomes a market order, the final price can be noticeably different from the stop price in a fast-moving market.

## Stop-limit order

A **stop-limit order** becomes a **limit order** when the stop price is reached. It gives price control but, like any limit order, may not execute.

## How long orders last

Unless specified otherwise, orders are usually **day orders** that expire at the end of the trading day. **Good-til-canceled** orders stay open until filled or canceled, subject to the broker's limits.

## Key takeaways

- Market orders favor speed; limit orders favor price.
- Stop orders turn into market orders when triggered, so the price isn't guaranteed.
- Not every broker offers every order type; check before trading.
