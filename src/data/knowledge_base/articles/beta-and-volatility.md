# Volatility and Beta

## Volatility

**Volatility** is how much and how quickly prices move up and down. The more dramatic the swings, the higher the volatility, and the potential risk. It is often measured by the **standard deviation** of returns: how widely returns typically spread around their average.

Different investors experience volatility differently:

- **Long-term, buy-and-hold investors** often treat day-to-day swings as background noise, focusing on growth over years or decades.
- **Investors who need money soon**, for a house or a car, can be hurt by volatility, because a drop could force them to sell at a loss. Less volatile options and diversification help.
- **Short-term traders** try to profit from volatility over days or even seconds.

## Beta

**Beta** measures how a stock tends to move **relative to the overall market**, not its total volatility. A benchmark index such as the S&P 500 is assigned a beta of 1.

- **Beta above 1:** the stock has historically moved more than the market. A beta of 1.2 means it has historically moved about 120% for every 100% move in the index.
- **Beta below 1:** it has historically moved less. A beta of 0.85 has moved less than the index.

**Example:** if the market falls 10%, a stock with a beta of 1.5 might be expected to fall about 15%, and one with a beta of 0.5 about 5%, based on their past behavior. Actual moves can differ.

Growth stocks generally have higher betas than value stocks. Higher beta comes with higher risk and the potential for higher returns; lower beta usually means lower risk and lower potential returns.

## Beta vs. total volatility

A stock can have **high volatility but a low beta** if its moves aren't linked to the market's, for example a company whose price swings on its own drug trial results. Beta captures **market risk**; standard deviation captures **total** ups and downs, including company-specific swings that diversification can reduce.

## Limits

Both measures are based on **past** prices, and a company's risk can change. Use them alongside other information, not as predictions.

## Key takeaways

- Volatility is the size of price swings; standard deviation is a common measure.
- Beta compares a stock's moves with the market's: above 1 is more sensitive, below 1 less.
- Both are backward-looking and don't predict future returns.
