# The Wash Sale Rule

This article describes the general US federal rule for investors (it doesn't apply to securities dealers). Whether two investments are "substantially identical" depends on the facts, so a tax professional can help with specific cases.

## What the rule says

You can't deduct a loss from selling stock or securities in a **wash sale**. A wash sale happens when you sell at a loss and, **within 30 days before or after the sale**, you:

1. buy substantially identical stock or securities;
2. acquire them in a fully taxable trade;
3. acquire a contract or option to buy them; or
4. acquire substantially identical stock for your IRA or Roth IRA.

It is also a wash sale if your spouse, or a corporation you control, buys substantially identical stock in that window. Counting both sides, the window spans 61 days.

## Why it exists

Without the rule, an investor could sell a losing position, claim the tax loss and immediately buy it back, keeping the same investment while lowering their tax bill. This matters most for **tax-loss harvesting**: selling investments at a loss to offset capital gains.

## The loss is postponed, not lost

A disallowed loss is added to the cost of the new shares, which becomes their **basis**. You get the benefit later, when you sell the new shares. The holding period of the new shares also includes the time you held the old ones.

**Example (from IRS Publication 550):** you buy 100 shares for $1,000 and sell them for $750, a $250 loss. Within 30 days you buy 100 shares of the same stock for $800. You can't deduct the $250 loss now; instead your basis in the new shares is $800 + $250 = $1,050.

The exception is a repurchase in an IRA or Roth IRA (case 4): the loss is disallowed and is not added to the basis, so its tax benefit is effectively lost.

## What counts as substantially identical

Stock of one company is ordinarily not substantially identical to stock of another company. A company's bonds or preferred stock usually aren't substantially identical to its common stock, unless, for example, they can be converted into the common stock and trade like it. The rule also covers options on the stock.

## Key takeaways

- Buying the same or a substantially identical investment within 30 days before or after a loss sale disallows the loss.
- The disallowed loss is normally added to the new shares' basis and recovered later.
- Repurchasing in an IRA or Roth IRA can make the loss permanently unusable.
