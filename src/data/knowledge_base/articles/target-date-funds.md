# Target Date Funds

## What a target date fund is

A **target date fund** holds a mix of investments, such as stocks and bonds, and gradually changes that mix over time. It's designed to make investing for retirement (or another goal) more convenient by diversifying your money and adjusting your **asset allocation** automatically.

The year in the name is the target date. A "2060 Fund" is designed for people who expect to retire around 2060. Target date funds are common in 401(k) plans, and some plans put workers in one automatically if they don't choose.

## How it works

A target date fund is usually a **fund of funds**: it owns other funds, such as stock and bond funds, rather than individual stocks and bonds. Its adviser shifts money from stock funds toward bond funds as the target date approaches. That planned shift is called the **glide path**.

**Example:** a 2060 fund might hold about 90% stocks today, for someone with decades until retirement, and gradually move toward a much larger share of bonds by 2060.

## "To" vs. "through" glide paths

- A **"to"** glide path stops changing the mix at the target date. These funds generally become conservative earlier.
- A **"through"** glide path keeps adjusting past the target date, staying more invested in stocks for longer, because retirement can last decades.

## Things to check

- **Funds with the same date can differ a lot.** Two 2040 funds may hold very different mixes and perform very differently, before and at the target date.
- **No guarantee.** You can lose money, including near or after the target date, and the fund doesn't guarantee enough income in retirement.
- **Fees.** You pay the fund's fees, which may include the fees of the underlying funds. Compare expense ratios.
- **Fit.** The fund assumes a typical investor retiring in that year; your own risk tolerance and other savings may call for something different.

Read the fund's prospectus, which describes its glide path, strategy and fees.

## Key takeaways

- A target date fund automatically shifts from stocks toward bonds as its target year approaches.
- Funds with the same target year can have very different investment mixes and risks.
- They aren't guaranteed, and fees vary, so read the prospectus.
