# Early Withdrawals from Retirement Accounts: The 10% Tax and Exceptions

This article describes general US federal rules; exceptions have detailed conditions, so check with IRS.gov or a tax professional before relying on one.

## The basic rule

Money withdrawn from an IRA or workplace retirement plan **before age 59½** is an "early" distribution. On top of regular income tax on the taxable amount, you generally owe an **additional 10% tax**, unless an exception applies. For SIMPLE IRAs, the additional tax can be **25%** during the first two years of participation.

**Example:** at age 40, you withdraw $20,000 from a traditional 401(k). If you're in the 22% bracket, you might owe about $4,400 of income tax plus a $2,000 additional tax, leaving roughly $13,600, and that money no longer grows for retirement.

## Common exceptions

Some apply to both IRAs and workplace plans; others to only one.

**Both IRAs and plans:**

- total and permanent **disability**, or death;
- unreimbursed **medical expenses** above 7.5% of adjusted gross income;
- **birth or adoption**: up to $5,000 per child;
- one **emergency personal expense** withdrawal a year: up to $1,000;
- victims of **domestic abuse**: up to the lesser of $10,000 or 50% of the account;
- **qualified disaster** recovery distributions, up to $22,000;
- a series of substantially equal periodic payments, and an IRS levy.

**IRAs only:**

- **first-time homebuyers**: up to $10,000;
- qualified **higher education** expenses;
- health insurance premiums while unemployed.

**Workplace plans only:**

- leaving your job **in or after the year you turn 55** (50 for certain public safety employees), for that employer's plan.

## Roth accounts

Roth IRA contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn at any time without tax or penalty. Earnings withdrawn early may be taxed and face the 10% additional tax unless an exception applies.

## Reporting

Use **Form 5329** to report the additional tax, or to claim an exception your plan didn't already record.

## Key takeaways

- Withdrawals before age 59½ generally owe income tax plus an additional 10% tax.
- Exceptions include disability, large medical bills, birth or adoption, and leaving a job at 55 or older (plans only).
- First-time homebuyer and education exceptions apply to IRAs, not 401(k)s.
